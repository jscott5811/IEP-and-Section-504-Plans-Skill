# Parent action checklist

The three steps the skill walks a parent through. Legal basis is in
[verified-sources.md](verified-sources.md); every step below is the plain-language
version of it.

## Step 1 — Gather

Collect before writing to the school:

- Medical diagnoses or doctor's letters about the disability
- Outside evaluations (private psychologist, therapist, medical)
- School records: report cards, progress reports, discipline records
- Work samples that show the struggle (and strengths)
- Your own notes: what you see at home, in your own words, dated

Why: the school must use a **variety of sources** — parent-provided records
count. A diagnosis alone is never enough for either law; the school must do
its own evaluation.

## Step 2 — Submit a written request

Write to the school district (principal and special education director).
Keep it short and dated. Include:

- Your child's name and date of birth
- That you are requesting an evaluation for special education (IDEA) and/or
  accommodations under Section 504
- A plain description of what you see at home and at school
- That you are attaching the records from Step 1
- Your signature and contact information

What happens next (federal rules):

- **IDEA:** the district needs your **written consent** before it evaluates.
  Once you consent, the evaluation must be finished within **60 days** (or
  your state's timeline). If the district refuses to evaluate, it must give
  you **written notice explaining why** — you can challenge that decision.
- **Section 504:** the district must evaluate in a **timely manner** any
  student who needs or is believed to need services.

### Sample letter (customize, do not send as-is)

> [Date]
>
> Dear [Principal / Special Education Director],
>
> I am writing to request an evaluation of my child, [name], date of birth
> [date], who attends [school, grade]. I am concerned that [1–2 plain
> sentences about what you see].
>
> I am requesting an evaluation to determine eligibility for special
> education and related services under the Individuals with Disabilities
> Education Act, and for accommodations under Section 504 of the
> Rehabilitation Act.
>
> I have attached [list records: doctor's letter dated X, report card,
> etc.]. Please send me the consent forms and the procedural safeguards
> notice.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your name, phone, email]

## Step 3 — Keep organized records

Date everything. Keep copies of:

- Every letter and email to and from the school
- Consent forms you sign (note the date — the 60-day clock starts here)
- Evaluation reports
- The IEP or 504 plan itself (keep the signed copy)
- Meeting notes (write yours right after each meeting)
- Progress reports on IEP goals
- Any notice the school sends you, especially refusals

## If the school says no

- Ask for the refusal **in writing** with reasons (required under IDEA).
- Ask for the district's **procedural safeguards** notice — it explains
  mediation, complaints, and due process.
- Contact your state's **Parent Training and Information Center** for free
  help. (This skill does not give legal advice.)
